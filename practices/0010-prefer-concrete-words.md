## Why

An abstraction (a category name, a generalization, a nominalized process) can be true of many different specific situations, so it forces the reader to reconstruct which specific situation the writer actually means. A concrete word — a number, a name, an observed input or output — carries that information directly, so the reader does not have to guess or ask a follow-up question.

## When it applies

Any explanation, description, or report. Applies most directly to describing what a system does or did: state the actual behavior (a number, a name, a specific action) rather than a category that behavior falls into.

## Examples

- Bad: "The system experienced a performance degradation under certain conditions."
- Good: "Response time rose from 80ms to 1.2s when more than 500 concurrent requests arrived."
